# ThinkBuddy — two-minute presentation demo

Use one story throughout: **“How can we make our school more eco-friendly?”**

## Timed script

| Time | Show / do | Say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:12 | Homepage; point to the mission prompt. | “Students and young builders often have an idea but struggle to decide what to do next. ThinkBuddy helps them explore different perspectives, challenge assumptions, and choose a practical next step.” |
| 0:12–0:25 | Open the prepared school mission. Briefly point to the context fields or saved context. | “Let’s ask: how can we make our school more eco-friendly? We can add our audience, budget, and deadline, so the discussion starts with our actual constraints.” |
| 0:25–0:45 | **Discuss**: open one mascot explanation, then show the reply composer and recipient selector. | “Each character brings a different perspective: students, the environment, practical planning, or critical questioning. I can reply to one character or the whole council, attach supporting material, and inspect explanations and sources. Voice input is available with live AI.” |
| 0:45–1:02 | **Breakdown**: show prepared issues; select **Focus here** on one issue. Point to suggested actions. | “Next, we break the problem into smaller issues. I can edit the assumptions and choose one focus, such as reducing lunch waste. Suggested actions include a purpose and completion criteria; I decide which ones to adopt.” |
| 1:02–1:25 | **Research**: show a prepared poll, its audience and budget; briefly open **Preview as respondent**. Close it and show prepared sample results. | “Now we turn that uncertainty into a test. ThinkBuddy supports polls, surveys, and prototype feedback, with questions, an audience, and a budget. This research flow is simulated today: responses are examples, and no money is spent. Summaries link back to the original sample responses.” |
| 1:25–1:43 | Show the completed decision fields and **Record decision**, or a previously saved decision in history. | “The final decision stays mine. For this walkthrough, I choose a small lunch-waste pilot. I record why, what remains unknown, and the next real-world test. The decision history preserves that reasoning so I can revisit it later.” |
| 1:43–2:00 | Return to **Discuss → Thinking receipt**; show takeaway, uncertainty, next action, and decision. Finish on the mascot board or receipt. | “Finally, the Thinking receipt brings together the takeaway, remaining uncertainty, next action, and my decision. I can save the discussion and return later. ThinkBuddy turns one big question into different perspectives, a focused test, and a decision I can explain.” |

## Prepare before presenting

- Create the school mission and complete one discussion, breakdown, research draft, simulated launch, and decision in advance. Generate a Thinking receipt after the decision is saved.
- Use prepared browser tabs for the landing page and saved mission. Keep a receipt tab ready as a fallback; use only one tab for edits.
- Rehearse navigation once. The spoken script is approximately 270 words; keep clicks moving while speaking. Do not wait for generation during the timed presentation.
- If using guest/demo mode, replace “Each character brings a different perspective” with “These demo characters illustrate different perspectives.” Replace the voice sentence with “Live mode also supports voice input.”
- If a response is delayed, say “Here is the prepared result of that step” and move to the saved state. At 1:43, go directly to the receipt.

## Scope of this demo

This flow follows the currently wired workspace: **Discuss → Breakdown → Research → decision → Thinking receipt**. It covers the primary journey and mentions secondary input features without spending time on settings or every control.

ThinkMap, Obsidian notes/export/sync, ThinkLab, and ThinkPath have code or documentation in the repository, but the current workspace does not expose their navigation. Do not promise a live click-through of those tools in this two-minute demo. Research recruitment and payments are also future functionality; the current research launch is simulated.
